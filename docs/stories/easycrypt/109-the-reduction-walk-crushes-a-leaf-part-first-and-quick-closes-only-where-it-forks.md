## 1. Why this story exists

With story 108, all five reduction hops of Full4WHS close with no admit, in 5917 s of EasyCrypt
time (hop 12: 2602 s, down from 7966 s). Two costs are left that the walk can avoid:

1. **A leaf part that plain `smt()` cannot close.** After `move: hpre => />.`, a reduction leaf
   still has 0–5 parts. Most of them are map equalities whose two sides differ only by `{1}` and
   `{2}`, for example `d_Keys_R.[kid{1}, d_U{1}, …] = d_Keys_R.[kid{2}, d_U{2}, …]`. `smt()` times
   out on them (about 4.4 s), `smt(<hints>)` too, and then the crush closes them in about 2 s. The
   crush moves the part's premises back into the goal, and `/>` substitutes their equalities.
2. **A quick close at an arm start that the walk did not need.** The walk tries `auto => /#.`
   (2 s cap) at the start of every arm, before it reads the heads. When the arm then goes on with
   exit-guard decisions only, down to a leaf, that quick close is wasted: the leaf tries its own
   quick close anyway.

## 3. What to build

### 3.1 A reduction leaf part starts with the crush

1. **For `Judgement::Reduction` only,** when the leaf procedure introduced premises, the part
   fallbacks are in this order: the crush (`move: <premises> => />; smt(<hints>).`, the sentence
   fallback 3 sends now), then `smt().`, then `smt(<hints>).`. The last of them is retried on a
   timeout (`retry_close`), as now; then the key case split, then the admit, as now.
2. With no premises, the order stays `smt().`, `smt(<hints>).`.
3. **Equivalences, side invariants and package invariants do not change:** `smt().`,
   `smt(<hints>).`, the crush, as now (stories 65, 86–93).
4. The fallback order comes from one pure function of the judgement and of whether there are
   premises, so that the tests can read it without EasyCrypt.

### 3.2 The quick close comes only where the walk forks, or at a leaf

5. **`walk` no longer tries the quick close at an arm start before it reads the heads.** It reads
   the heads (`full_front`, `classify`) first.
6. **The quick close comes before the step of these heads** (once per head, at an arm start or
   not):
   - `Leaf`: as now (`leaf`);
   - `Branches`: before the `sp` and `if.` of `branches`;
   - `Samplings`: before the `sp` and `seq 1 1` of `samplings`;
   - `Mismatch`: before the admit, so that an arm the quick close can close is not admitted.
7. **No quick close before `AssumptionGuard`, `TailBranch` and `ExitGuards`:** their `rcond`s keep
   one program goal, and the walk goes on to the next head, where item 6 applies. So the quick
   close still comes before every point where the walk forks, on a goal with the decided `if`s
   removed.
8. The quick close of a side goal, its 2 s cap on a program goal, and the equivalence algorithm's
   quick close (`step_node`, at every inner node of the joint tree) do not change.
9. Which heads get the quick close comes from one pure function of `Head`.

### 3.3 Docs

10. `docs/proving-algorithm.md` §10.1: the quick close comes before a fork (`if.`, `seq`), a leaf or
    a mismatch, not at an arm start; §10.2 or §6: a reduction leaf part starts with the crush.

## 6. Rejected alternatives

- **No quick close at any arm start, only at leaves and side goals.** It would save about 1350 s,
  but an arm that the quick close closes at once could then be walked through all its branches.
  Rejected by the owner, 2026-11-14.
- **Skip `smt().` on a reduction leaf part.** The crush closed every part it was tried on, but
  `smt().` stays after it in case it fails on a part that `smt()` closes.
- **The crush first for every judgement.** An equivalence part goes through Domino's verdict and the
  tuned fallback sequence (stories 86–93); the equivalence algorithm does not change (the owner,
  2026-11-13).
- **Raise or drop the 2 s cap.** It is shared with the equivalence algorithm, and the margin
  depends on the machine's load (story 108 §6).
